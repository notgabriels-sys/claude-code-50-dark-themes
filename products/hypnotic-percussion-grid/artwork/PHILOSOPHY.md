# Rhythmic Lattice

Rhythmic Lattice treats percussion as a system of small events whose meaning comes from position, repetition and interruption. The central structure is a sixteen-column field crossed by four families, a restrained reference to the product's 16-by-4 architecture and to step sequencing without depicting software or hardware. Every cell must feel deliberately placed, with the measured confidence of a master practitioner.

The composition is built from contrast between strict order and microscopic deviation. Most of the grid remains dormant; selected marks flare, stretch or break alignment in response to the actual audition-reel spectrum. Rhythm is therefore visible as density and absence, not illustrated with familiar musical symbols. Its craft should suggest countless hours spent tuning intervals, line weights and optical balance.

Colour operates as signal state. Carbon black forms the silent ground, mineral ivory establishes the lattice, and one heated orange records active events. Orange is concentrated rather than decorative, so every bright cell carries consequence. Subtle graphite changes beneath the grid should reward close inspection while preserving immediate clarity at storefront scale.

Typography behaves like technical annotation. The title is decisive but contained, while the artist, file count and format sit on the same invisible measurement system as the grid. Letterspacing, baseline relationships and margins are painstakingly resolved; no text may appear pasted over the visual field or compete with the rhythmic structure.

The finished object should feel like an archival plate for an imaginary percussion instrument: exact, tactile and emotionally restrained. Its apparent simplicity conceals meticulous construction, from the embedded spectral evidence to the alignment of every active step. The result must feel authored and laboured over, never generated by a generic template.
