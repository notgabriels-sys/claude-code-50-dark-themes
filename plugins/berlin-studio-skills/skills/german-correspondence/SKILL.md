# German correspondence

Output here is a finished document he can send without editing. Not a lesson.
If he wants to understand *why* a case is used, that is
`german-practice-drills`.

## Always ask yourself first: Sie or du?

Get this wrong and the whole text reads wrong.

- **Sie** — authorities, Finanzamt, landlords, insurers, banks, new clients,
  venue and label contacts you have not met, anyone older in a formal role.
  Capitalise `Sie`, `Ihnen`, `Ihr`.
- **du** — Berlin club, label, and studio culture is genuinely informal.
  Promoters, bookers, artists, and engineers usually write `du` from the first
  message. Switching to `Sie` there reads as cold or sarcastic.

When unsure with a music-industry contact writing in German: **mirror whatever
they used**. If they opened, they set the register.

## Structure of a formal letter

```
Absender (name, street, PLZ Ort)

Empfänger (name, department, street, PLZ Ort)

Ort, den TT.MM.JJJJ

Betreff: <concrete, no greeting, no verb needed>

Sehr geehrte Frau <Nachname>, / Sehr geehrter Herr <Nachname>,

<body — first sentence states the request or the fact, not pleasantries>

Mit freundlichen Grüßen

<Unterschrift>
<Name in Druckbuchstaben>

Anlagen: <list>
```

Name unknown → `Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,`. Never `Liebe/r` to an
authority.

Note the comma after the salutation, and that the body then **starts
lower-case** unless the first word is a noun or a proper name. This is the
single most common mistake by English speakers.

## Register ladder

| Situation | Opening | Closing |
|---|---|---|
| Authority, formal | `Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,` | `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` |
| Known business contact | `Sehr geehrte Frau Meyer,` | `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` / `Beste Grüße` |
| Warm professional | `Hallo Frau Meyer,` | `Viele Grüße` / `Beste Grüße` |
| Club / label / peer | `Hallo Jan,` / `Hi Jan,` | `Liebe Grüße` / `Beste Grüße` / `LG` |

`Hochachtungsvoll` is archaic and reads as hostile. Do not use it.

## Formulas that carry weight

- Opening a request: `hiermit bitte ich Sie um …` / `ich wende mich an Sie, weil …`
- Referring back: `Bezug nehmend auf Ihr Schreiben vom TT.MM.JJJJ …`
- Attaching: `Anbei sende ich Ihnen …` / `Im Anhang finden Sie …`
- Deadline, firm but neutral: `Ich bitte Sie, mir bis zum TT.MM.JJJJ … zukommen zu lassen.`
- Chasing payment: `Trotz meiner Rechnung vom … ist bislang kein Zahlungseingang zu verzeichnen.`
- Objecting: `Hiermit lege ich Widerspruch gegen … vom TT.MM.JJJJ ein.`
- Cancelling: `Hiermit kündige ich meinen Vertrag … fristgerecht zum nächstmöglichen Termin.`
- Closing politely: `Für Rückfragen stehe ich Ihnen gerne zur Verfügung.`

## Letter types with legal weight — get these exactly right

**Kündigung.** State it is a Kündigung, name the contract and any customer
number, and say `fristgerecht zum nächstmöglichen Termin` rather than naming a
date you might have miscalculated. Always request written confirmation:
`Bitte bestätigen Sie mir die Kündigung schriftlich unter Angabe des
Beendigungsdatums.` Send by post with proof for anything that matters.

**Widerspruch.** Deadlines are short and strict — commonly one month from
receipt. Lodging it in time matters more than reasoning it fully; the reasons
can follow. Always name the decision and its date.

**Mahnung.** Escalate in steps: neutral reminder → Mahnung with a firm date →
notice of further steps. Keep the tone flat throughout. Anger costs you the
client and does not speed up payment.

## Formatting conventions that mark a text as native

- Dates `TT.MM.JJJJ` (`24.08.2026`), never US order.
- Decimal comma, thousands point: `1.234,56 €`, with the € after the number.
- Times 24-hour: `20:00 Uhr`.
- `ß` after long vowels and diphthongs (`Straße`, `Grüße`) — but Swiss texts
  use `ss`. Berlin: use `ß`.
- Nouns capitalised. Every one.

## Working method

1. Establish the recipient and the register before writing a word.
2. Ask for anything you would otherwise invent — reference numbers, contract
   numbers, dates, addresses. Never fabricate a Kundennummer or an Aktenzeichen.
3. Produce the German text as the deliverable.
4. Add a short English gloss underneath **only** if he asks or if the letter
   carries legal consequence he should understand before sending.
